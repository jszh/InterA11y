Is the rally running out of road?

By R. Okafor · 8 min read · Updated 2 hours ago

For eighteen months the index has done little but climb. Each pullback has been shallow, each dip swiftly bought. To the bulls this is the hallmark of a durable expansion: earnings broadening out beyond a handful of mega-caps, credit spreads placid, the consumer stubbornly solvent. To the bears it is the late, giddy stage of a cycle that has simply not yet been handed its bill.

The strongest argument for the optimists is breadth. A year ago the advance was narrow to the point of fragility — strip out the seven largest names and the market was flat. That is no longer true. Industrials, financials, and even the long-suffering small-cap cohort have begun to participate, which historically is what a healthy, self-sustaining advance looks like rather than a speculative blow-off.

And yet valuation is doing a great deal of heavy lifting. On a cyclically adjusted basis the market sits in the richest decile of its own history. Rich does not mean doomed — expensive markets can stay expensive for years — but it does compress the margin for error. When prices already assume a soft landing, a flawless one, there is little cushion left if the data merely disappoints rather than collapses.

The honest answer is that nobody ringing the bell at the top has ever done so on schedule. The reader who skips to the bottom for a verdict will be disappointed: there is no verdict, only a balance of probabilities that shifts a little with every print of inflation and every earnings call. What follows is an attempt to weigh them.
