Chapter 1: Introduction

Tidal salt marshes buffer coastlines against storm surge, sequester carbon at rates exceeding most terrestrial forests, and provide nursery habitat for commercial fish species. Aldgate Bay lost an estimated 62% of its vegetated marsh between 1965 and 2010 to subsidence and dredging. This report documents a four-year restoration trial across eleven plots.

Chapter 2: Methods

We re-graded subsided plots to target elevations, installed brushwood sediment fences, and planted nursery-grown Spartina alterniflora plugs at 0.5 m spacing. Vegetation cover, accretion, and macroinvertebrate counts were measured quarterly using fixed quadrats and sediment pins.

Chapter 3: Results

Mean vegetation cover rose from 8% at baseline to 71% by month 36. Sediment accretion averaged 9.4 mm/yr in fenced plots versus 2.1 mm/yr in controls. Macroinvertebrate density in restored plots reached 84% of reference-marsh levels by the final survey. Two plots in the highest-energy zone failed to establish and were excluded after month 18.

Chapter 4: Discussion

The strong accretion response suggests sediment supply, not planting density, was the limiting factor at this site. Failure in the high-energy zone indicates that wave attenuation must precede planting where fetch exceeds ~1.2 km.

Chapter 5: Conclusions

Brushwood-fenced replanting restored functional marsh on nine of eleven plots within three years and is recommended for low-to-moderate-energy reaches of Aldgate Bay. High-energy reaches require prior breakwater works. We recommend continued monitoring through year ten to confirm persistence.
