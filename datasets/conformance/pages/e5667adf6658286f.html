The City Loop is finally getting weekend service

By Marisol Tan · Updated 7:40 a.m. · 6 min read

After three years of pilot studies and two stalled budget cycles, the Metropolitan Transit Authority voted Tuesday to extend the City Loop tram to a full seven-day schedule beginning in September, ending a long-running gap that left weekend riders stranded between the harbor district and the university.

The 4–1 board vote authorizes $2.1 million in operating funds for the first year, drawn from a reserve account that had been earmarked for a since-cancelled ferry expansion. Two additional tram sets, currently in storage, will be returned to service to cover the added runs.

"This is the line people actually use to get to their weekend shifts," said board member Anita Cho, who has pushed for the change since 2022. "We were treating it like a commuter-only route, and that never matched the ridership data."

Service will run from 6 a.m. to 11 p.m. on Saturdays and Sundays, with trams every 20 minutes during the day and every 35 minutes after 8 p.m. The authority said it would revisit frequency in the spring once weekend ridership is measured.

Riders' advocates welcomed the decision but cautioned that the harbor stop still lacks a covered platform — a separate project that remains unfunded.
