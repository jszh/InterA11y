Brewing basics

The Two-Number Pour-Over

If you remember only two numbers, you can make a good cup of filter coffee anywhere: a ratio of 1 gram of coffee to 16 grams of water, and a total brew time of about three minutes. Everything else — grinder, kettle, fancy dripper — is refinement on top of those two anchors.

Weigh the coffee, weigh the water, watch the clock. That is the whole craft, distilled.

For a single mug, that means roughly 22 grams of coffee to 350 grams of water. Grind medium, about the texture of coarse sand. Start a timer as you pour, wet the grounds with twice their weight in water (around 45 g) and wait 30 seconds for the bloom — the bed will puff up as trapped gas escapes. Then pour the rest in slow, steady spirals, finishing your pour by 2:00 and your drawdown by 3:00.

Taste, then adjust by feel: sour and thin means grind finer or brew longer; bitter and harsh means grind coarser or brew shorter. Hold the two numbers steady and change just one variable at a time.
