The slow return of the salt marsh

By Marisol Quenby · 18 min read

For a century the estuary was diked, drained, and forgotten. Then a single broken culvert let the tide back in, and the marsh began to remember what it had been.

Restoration ecologists now argue that the cheapest climate infrastructure may be the land we already ruined — if only we let the water decide where to go.
