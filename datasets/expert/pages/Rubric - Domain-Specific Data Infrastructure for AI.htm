    The era of experience

    In most domains, shaping experience is trivial - a game has a score, a compiler has a test. But the knowledge that drives outcomes in the world's most complex domains was never written down. Some of it is judgment - clinical reasoning, scientific intuition, decisions that can't be looked up. Some of it is skill - procedural expertise in physical environments, captured in gesture and action, not text. It lives in demonstration, in the gap between what experts say and what they actually do.

    We call this the non-verifiable knowledge problem. Designing the reward signal, the environment, the evaluation criteria for domains with no scoreboard that is the unsolved problem. Solving it requires new research, not better pipelines.

    Our Research Pillars

    Four classical orders of inquiry —

    each essential, each reinforcing the others.

    I

    Expert Signal Elicitation

    We study how to extract training signals from what experts do, not just what they say — across modalities, languages, and cultures.

    II

    Complex Reasoning Environments

    We build RL environments grounded in real expert decision-making, for tasks where rule-based reward breaks down.

    III

    Epistemic Evaluation

    We design evaluation frameworks for domains where ground truth is contested and capability must be inferred from behavior.

    IV

    Multi-Modal Signal Integration

    We capture and structure knowledge across voice, image, gesture, and action — and structure it into cross-modal datasets built for post-training.
